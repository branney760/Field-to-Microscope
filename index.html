THREE SPRINGS · CALIFORNIA CONNECTIONS
Scientific boundaries & sources

Three Springs and all sample labels, conditions, images, and organism identifications are fictional teaching constructions. No image is a research micrograph. The archaeal, cyanobacterial, diatom, and yeast specimens are generic representatives; the red alga is a simplified model inspired by Cyanidioschyzon merolae. Shape or size alone does not distinguish Bacteria from Archaea. Not seeing a nucleus is not proof that a cell is prokaryotic. The structural models show features that routine light microscopy cannot resolve.

The nuclear-marker view illustrates a prepared, nucleus-targeted fluorescence method for the algal and yeast specimens; it is not a general DNA stain. Its false color marks nuclei and does not show all cellular DNA. Simplified structural diagrams are not to scale. Sample occurrence is not proof of active growth or a measured tolerance limit.

Background: Siering, Clarke & Wilson (2006), Geochemical and Biological Diversity of Acidic, Hot Springs in Lassen Volcanic National Park, DOI: 10.1080/01490450500533916. Mills et al. (2026), Prokaryotic and microbial eukaryotic communities across acid-sulfate and chloride-rich hot springs in Lassen Volcanic National Park, DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-61095-4. National Park Service: Lassen astrobiology.

Related research supports the instructional background; the exercise does not reproduce its raw data or measure environmental tolerance.
